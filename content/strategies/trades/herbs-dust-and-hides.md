I spent a wet week in the Wetlands picking everything that grew out of the mud. I dislike rain and I dislike mud, and the Wetlands are made of both. When I sold the lot in Ironforge, the Kingsblood had paid for the week and the Peacebloom I'd picked in Loch Modan on the way had paid for the beer.

## Why it pays
Forever added hundreds of recipes, and they decide what sells. Up to skill 225, {item:3356} goes into nine of the new ones, more than any other herb, all nine for alchemists. On the beta one Kingsblood fetched more than a stack of {item:765}.

Enchanting turns the green items you loot into dust and essences. No vendor pays a copper for them, so all of it goes to the auction house, and the new recipes ask most often for {item:11082}, in twenty-five of them, eighteen for the tailors' new cloth sets.

Skinning gives leather and now and then a hide. No new recipe takes a raw hide, but 113 of them take a cured one, and leatherworkers cure their own. On the beta a {item:4232} sold for two or three times what a {item:2319} did.

{plate}

## What you need
Herbalism and Skinning you have from the page before. Journeyman at skill 50 costs 5 silver and Expert at 125 costs 50, and the two take you to 225. Enchanting would be a third profession, so it goes on an alt or replaces a gatherer later. Few trainers teach Expert Enchanting. In the data they are {npc:11072} in Elwynn and {npc:11074} in Stonetalon.

## Where
Kingsblood and {item:3355} grow all over the zones of the 20s, in the Wetlands, Hillsbrad, Ashenvale, Stonetalon, Duskwood and the Barrens. {item:3357} grows mostly in the Wetlands, Hillsbrad and Ashenvale. Below level 20 the herbs are only worth picking when they stand in your way.

## How
1. Level with Herbalism and Skinning through the zones of the 20s.
2. Keep the hides apart from the leather and sell them as they are.
3. Put the green items you loot in the bank instead of selling them to a vendor. Mail them to an enchanter later.
4. Sell the herbs of the 20s as you go. The low ones keep, and a new alchemist will want them for skill.
5. When cloth is cheap, the travellers buy it, make something from it, disenchant that and sell the dust. Do the sums at the auction house before you try it.

{live}

## What can go wrong
Dust has no vendor price, so on a quiet day it sits in your bank. Herb prices follow the alchemists, and every other herbalist in the zone is after the same Kingsblood. The shuffle only works while the cloth costs less than the dust it turns into, and both prices move fast on a new server. The travellers also advise against Enchanting at the start, except to make yourself a wand, because it costs money to level.

## Verdict
Herbalism paid me best of the three. Enchanting I'll leave to an alt with more patience than I have.
